About

Research summary

Ph.D. candidate in Structural Biology with expertise spanning experimental and computational methods for characterizing disease-associated proteins. Current research focuses on the structure and function of a therapeutic target protein implicated in Alzheimer's disease and its interaction with amyloid precursor protein (APP), combining cryo-electron microscopy, X-ray crystallography, biophysical characterization (ITC, FPLC/SEC), and computational drug discovery (molecular docking, molecular dynamics, MM-GBSA, ADME profiling).

Experienced in solving cryo-EM structures end-to-end — from data collection troubleshooting through refinement — and in running iterative, hypothesis-driven computational-to-experimental research loops.

Research

Where the work sits

Three interlocking threads: structure determination, biophysical characterization, and computation — run as one iterative loop rather than separate tracks.

Structure Determination

Cryo-EM & X-ray crystallography

End-to-end cryo-EM pipeline in cryoSPARC — template picking, 2D/3D classification, 3D variability analysis, and preferred-orientation mitigation — followed by DeepEMhancer post-processing and Phenix real-space refinement. Parallel crystallization screening and X-ray diffraction data processing.

Biophysics

Binding & dimerization

ITC-based characterization of protein dimerization and small-molecule interactions, including thermogram troubleshooting and quantitative fitting in PEAQ-ITC. Dimerization mechanisms validated via SDS-PAGE and Western blotting.

Computation

Drug discovery workflows

Glide XP/SP docking, Desmond MD (up to 1000 ns), Prime MM-GBSA and Induced Fit Docking, QikProp ADME prediction, and Jaguar QM/MM analysis across independent inhibitor discovery programs — plus integrative structural modeling of multi-protein complexes using IMP.

In development

Structural homolog & drug-repurposing lookup tool

A public web tool where a user submits a protein sequence, which is searched against BLAST for structurally and sequence-similar proteins, then cross-referenced against known drug–target databases to shortlist drugs already reported against related targets — turning a raw sequence into a ranked list of candidate, previously validated compounds worth investigating.
